# 2026-09-15 — From Sheep Camps to Supper
## The Diaz Family Nobody Told You About

### Episode description
Avel Diaz’s oral histories connect Basque immigration, South End sheep camps, Crane High School, underground Dutch-oven cooking, Burns small business, and his youngest son Tony’s restaurant. The episode follows the Diaz family as a working Harney County network rather than a cradle-to-grave biography, and ends with two open research leads: Tony’s Outward Bound connection and surviving memories of the downtown restaurant.

### Cold open
A restaurant story that begins with sagebrush, sheep camps, sourdough, and a father who learned to bake bread underground.

### Source ledger
1. **Harney County History Project, AV-Oral History #276, Avel Diaz — With Video**, May 17, 1990. Primary narrative source. Covers Avel’s family origins, childhood, Andrews, Crane, sheep work, camp cooking, Burns business, children, Tony’s cooking interest, City of Burns work and restaurant path.
2. **AV-Oral History #297, Avel Diaz — Discusses South End Basque Sheepherders**, July 24, 1991. Primary narrative source for South End sheep outfits, Basque hotels, grazing patterns, named families and the Basque visitor at Tony’s restaurant.
3. **AV-Oral History #355, Florence Scharff — With Video**, March 14, 1994. Independent supporting oral history. Mentions Tony Diaz in connection with Outward Bound visits to a remote cabin; exact role remains unresolved.
4. **Avel Diaz on Basque Heritage, #488, 1979.** Supporting source identified in the Harney County Library oral-history index; project notes record Tony/Anthony as the youngest child and working at the Burns sawmill at that time.

### Claim ledger
- Avel Diaz was born in Mountain Home, Idaho, in 1921; parents Beatriz and Emilio were from Lekeitio, Spain. **Type:** firsthand recollection. **Source:** #276. **Confidence:** high for recollection.
- Avel’s mother died when he was an infant and the children were placed with different Basque families. **Type:** firsthand family recollection. **Source:** #276. **Confidence:** high as autobiographical memory.
- Avel came to Andrews as a boy, stayed in Harney County, worked in sheep camps and attended Crane High School. **Type:** firsthand recollection. **Source:** #276. **Confidence:** high.
- Principal Mr. Haberly enabled Avel to finish school after room-and-board problems. **Type:** firsthand recollection. **Source:** #276. **Confidence:** high as remembered experience.
- Avel learned practical sheep-camp cooking using Dutch ovens, pits and sagebrush coals. **Type:** firsthand recollection. **Source:** #276. **Confidence:** high.
- Tony learned sourdough/cooking at home, declined cooking school, worked on golf courses and for the City of Burns caring for park/cemetery, then pursued a restaurant in Burns. **Type:** firsthand parent recollection. **Source:** #276. **Confidence:** high as Avel’s account.
- Tony’s restaurant connected Avel with a visiting Basque-speaking family from the Emmett area. **Type:** firsthand recollection. **Source:** #297. **Confidence:** high.
- Florence Scharff mentions Tony Diaz while discussing Outward Bound visits; the transcript does not establish his exact role. **Type:** independent recollection. **Source:** #355. **Confidence:** high for mention, unresolved for role.
- Project notes from #488 place Anthony/Tony as the youngest child working at the Burns sawmill in 1979. **Type:** oral-history index/project extraction. **Confidence:** medium until full #488 transcript is reviewed directly.
