# 2026-09-18 — Camp Harney
## The Fort Before the County

### Episode description
Ralph Dickenson’s Camp Harney research turns an old military-site story into a logistics story: Camp Steele on Rattlesnake Creek, water and grass, lumber, training, school debates, long mail delays, winter isolation, and the violent military system surrounding the post. The episode distinguishes Dickenson’s documented post-return research from his own speculation and sets up the 1878 conflict episode.

### Cold open
Camp Harney got a post office in 1874. Mail still took about thirteen days to The Dalles and roughly a month to Washington, D.C. Winter access could be reduced to snowshoes.

### Source ledger
1. **Harney County History Project, AV-Oral History #62-A, Ralph Dickenson — Camp Harney**, 1978, Harney County Historical Society. Primary narrative research source: Dickenson reads/discusses post returns, letters and reports and explicitly marks some claims as speculation.
2. **AV-Oral History #44-A — Camp Harney / Ralph Dickenson**, 1972. Earlier research summary with notes on garrison duty, Alcatraz military prisoners, 1867 operations and Army reports describing violence against Native people.

### Claim ledger
- Company K, 23rd Infantry established Camp Steele on Rattlesnake Creek on August 16, 1867, after marching from Camp Wright. **Type:** post return read by Dickenson. **Source:** #62A. **Confidence:** high within the cited record.
- First post commander identified as Lt. Goodale. **Type:** Dickenson’s record-based research. **Source:** #62A. **Confidence:** high.
- Crook had used a temporary Rattlesnake Camp in the area earlier; Dickenson associates it with 1864 reconnaissance. **Type:** later research. **Source:** #62A. **Confidence:** medium-high.
- Water and grass likely influenced site choice. **Type:** Dickenson’s explicit speculation. **Source:** #62A. **Confidence:** interpretive, not established fact.
- Ordinary duty was largely garrison/training duty; soldiers complained about isolation. **Type:** Dickenson’s synthesis of accounts. **Source:** #62A. **Confidence:** medium-high.
- A school debate appears in correspondence involving officer Hunt and enlisted man Randall; Dickenson had not found proof of a school at that point. **Type:** record-based research. **Source:** #62A. **Confidence:** medium-high.
- Robie’s Sawmill was probably north of camp, but Dickenson explicitly says the exact placement was not conclusively proven. **Type:** supported inference. **Source:** #62A. **Confidence:** medium.
- Post office established in 1874; cited report gives about thirteen days to The Dalles and about a month to Washington, with severe winter isolation. **Type:** Dickenson citing a report. **Source:** #62A. **Confidence:** high as report content.
- Camp Harney was not under siege as far as Dickenson could determine. **Type:** later research conclusion. **Source:** #62A. **Confidence:** medium-high.
- During the 1878 conflict, Dickenson cites reports of roughly 300–350 men and multiple military elements at the post. **Type:** later research summary of reports. **Source:** #62A. **Confidence:** medium-high.
- #44A summarizes Army reports describing killings of Native men and taking women/children prisoner. **Type:** military-report content relayed by later researcher. **Source:** #44A. **Confidence:** high that Dickenson reported this; broader context requires Indigenous and additional documentary sources.
- #44A notes soldiers with penitentiary sentences being sent to Alcatraz. **Type:** later research summary. **Source:** #44A. **Confidence:** medium-high.

### Timeline
- 1864 — Dickenson says Crook used a temporary Rattlesnake Camp during reconnaissance.
- Aug. 16, 1867 — Camp Steele established on Rattlesnake Creek by Company K, 23rd Infantry.
- 1874 — post office established at Camp Harney according to Dickenson’s cited report.
- 1878 — post becomes heavily used during the Bannock War/1878 conflict; Dickenson cites 300–350 personnel and multiple military elements.
- After post abandonment — Dickenson says the post office was moved to Harney City.

### Teacher one-sheet
**Core question:** Why would the U.S. Army build and maintain a remote post at Camp Harney, and what does its daily operation reveal about geography and government?
**Source-literacy focus:** Identify when Ralph Dickenson is citing a record, summarizing research, or explicitly speculating.
**Geography:** Distance to Canyon City/The Dalles, water/grass availability, winter isolation and supply routes.

### Student questions

4. Why is “used during a war” different from “under siege”?
5. What additional sources are required to understand Indigenous experiences of Camp Harney?
